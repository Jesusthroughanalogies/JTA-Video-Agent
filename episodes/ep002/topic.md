# Ep 2: Checkpoint A (topic), 4 Nov 2026

**Question:** If God is real and good, why is there so much suffering? (teased at the end of Ep 1)

**Angle:** Same honest format as Ep 1. Three pictures, the strongest sceptic reply to each, then Jesus. We don't claim to fully solve it; we show why suffering doesn't disprove God, and what Christianity says God did about it.

## The three pictures

1. **The puppet (why people can hurt each other).** A puppet-maker can make puppets that always smile and bow, but they can't love him back. Love needs freedom, and freedom means people can choose harm. Ancient: a Greek puppeteer with string marionettes (they existed in the ancient world).
   - Sceptic reply: "Then why earthquakes and disease? Nobody chose those." Answer: a world with fixed rules (fire warms and burns, water carries boats and drowns; ties back to the Ep 1 dials) plus the Bible's picture of a world that is broken and "groaning" (Romans 8:22). Admit openly that this is the hardest part.
2. **The tapestry (why it doesn't make sense to us).** From underneath, a weaving is knots and loose threads. From above, it's a picture. We see the underside. Joseph's story: sold by his brothers, then "You intended to harm me, but God intended it for good" (Genesis 50:20).
   - Sceptic reply: "That's a cop-out. You can explain anything that way." Answer: it isn't proof, but if a God exists who is big enough to make the universe, it would be strange if we could see all His reasons. Not knowing why is different from there being no why.
3. **The king who walks into the plague (God isn't distant from it).** A king could send help from the palace, or walk into the sick city himself. Christianity says God did the second. Jesus wept at His friend's grave (John 11:35), was betrayed, beaten and crucified. "A man of suffering, and familiar with pain" (Isaiah 53:3).
   - Payoff: the story ends with "He will wipe every tear from their eyes" (Revelation 21:4).

## Sources

- C.S. Lewis, *The Problem of Pain* (1940): free will and a world with fixed rules.
- Alvin Plantinga, *God, Freedom, and Evil* (1974): the free will defence; most philosophers now accept the "logical" problem of evil fails.
- Sceptic: Bart Ehrman, *God's Problem* (2008), and J.L. Mackie, "Evil and Omnipotence" (*Mind*, 1955). The old Epicurus-style trilemma ("Is God willing but not able...?") is shown and answered fairly.
